Weigh Out

First-person multiplayer platformerUnreal Engine 5Lead designerJul 2026–Present

Wizards are locked in an ancient prison, and only one clan can escape. Teams race to the top while shooting each other, but nobody takes damage. Shots change weight instead.

Pillars
Chaos, mastery, comeback, tension.

The core idea: weight

The problem: the game is a race. I wanted players to interfere with each other without damage or elimination, so combat stays part of movement.

The solution: every shot transfers weight on a scale from 0 to 100. The shooter gets lighter and the target gets heavier.

  • A basic shot: the shooter loses 20, the target gains 18.
  • A homing projectile: the target gains 60, the shooter loses 30.
  • Lighter players jump higher, fall slower and are harder to launch off a platform. Heavier players do the opposite.

Why it works: every shot is a trade-off. Hitting a rival helps you and hurts them, but it spends mana and exposes you to return fire.

Supporting mechanics

  • Charger stations: standing on one lowers your weight over time, and a timed button press every 2 seconds gives a bonus drop. A non-combat way to climb.
  • Lasers: static lasers add 30 weight once, and pulsing lasers add weight while you stand in them.
  • Launcher bomb: the core movement tool. Launches everyone near the blast, with power fading by distance.
  • Homing projectile: a comeback tool picked up from a white mist. One use, easier to aim, bounces once off a wall.
  • Pull: pull a teammate forward, drag a rival off the final platform, or yank yourself toward fixed objects.
  • Shield: drawn with the mouse. It blocks shots and pulls, and can be drawn behind you so a launch doesn’t knock you off.
  • Dash: a short burst for platforming.

Level design: the vertical level

The level escalates in three floors, each adding a new pressure.

  1. Floor 1: two starting rooms, one per team. Teams can only shoot each other through windows.
  2. Floor 2: the first meeting point. Platforms around a circle leave the center open for fights, and they re-arrange every round so players can’t memorize jumps.
  3. Floor 3: a long jump that needs weight 30 or less, so players must fight or use a charger first.
  4. Final platform: hold it for 15 seconds to win the round. If a rival arrives, the timer stops and you fight again.

When a player passes through the red mist, lava starts rising from the bottom. It reaches the top of floor 3 in about 2 minutes.

Playtest round 1: fixing a repetitive loop

Problem: the first prototype was one loop on repeat: shoot, get lighter, launch, climb. Testers lost interest quickly.

Evidence: two playtest rounds with up to 4 testers: casual players, a non-gamer and a professional FPS player.

What I changed:

  • Split the level into escalating floors, each with a different job.
  • Added charger stations so weight loss isn’t only about shooting.
  • Added rising lava, so falling now costs a life instead of just a climb.
  • Changed the win condition to a 15-second hold, giving trailing players a final fight.

Playtest round 2: making the game readable

Problems: players didn’t know where the goal was, and they forgot the launcher bomb was their main way to move.

  • Opening camera sweep: shows the goal at the start of every round.
  • Launcher icon on the HUD: a constant reminder of the core ability.
  • Breakable cell wall: each player starts sealed in a cell and must blast the wall with a launcher, which launches them out. The tutorial is the mechanic itself.
  • Weight scale HUD: redesigned from a plain bar into a scale icon with numbers, so it’s clear that weight (not health) is what changes.

I direct the project and own all gameplay and level design. I also create the character and animation, make the VFX and SFX, design the UI, and script some gameplay in Blueprints.

I designed it for broad appeal: cute characters, funny guns that stack on the head, a mouth that moves with audio input, and proximity voice chat.

The map and player choice

A circular map has a central boss room and 8 surrounding rooms, connected by speed-boost pipes. Everyone starts with only basic bullets and chooses: rush the boss, collect abilities first, or disrupt the other team.

The rooms come in two types: combat rooms that drop guns, and platforming challenges that grant abilities such as dash, shield, wall-walking and swinging. Harder challenges give stronger abilities.

Shield and gravity abilities

  • Shield (UGC and Weigh Out): in UGC, several enemy bots firing different projectile patterns at once made dodging almost impossible. My solution was a shield you draw by hand, shaped to match the incoming shots, which leaves room for player creativity.
  • Gravity abilities (UGC only): UGC is a PvPvE action platformer, so I added gravity tools to give players different ways to move: one lets you swing, the other lets you walk on walls and other surfaces. Players earn abilities by beating platforming challenges, but at the gravity challenge they must choose one of the two.

Technical design: hybrid physics

To get a funny, physics-driven look without losing control or performance, I simulate physics only on the spine and upper body while the feet and hips stay kinematic.

I also solved Steam multiplayer connection failures in recent Unreal versions (caused by App ID 480) with a workaround that restored client-host connections.

Burn and regrowth

Burn: you must light yourself to progress, so burning is the core mechanic and the main source of tension.

Relief: I didn’t want constant stress, so some puzzles put you out and pause the burn. Enemies exist to stall you and speed up burnout by stunning you.

Regrowth: defeating enemies makes you grow again. A timed finish button chains attacks into a combo, which defeats enemies faster. Some enemies, such as teeth with braces, can only be broken by timed finishes.

Postmortem: why I canceled it

After 5 months, team and outside playtests showed the gameplay loop didn’t work, and the scope was too big for our experience.

  • Test the core loop early, before building content around it.
  • Scope to the team you have.
  • Cutting a project is a design decision, not a failure.

What I did next: Weigh Out started as a 6-day jam prototype, was playtested early, and was reshaped twice before we committed to a release.
